# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two primary visitors, weighted equally:

- **Tech recruiters** scanning a pool of candidates. They spend 30-60 seconds deciding whether to pass or pursue. They want to see: role clarity, tech stack fit, evidence of real shipped work, and something that feels like a person rather than a template.
- **Startup founders and small business owners** looking to hire freelance or contract development. They want trust first, competence second. They need to feel like Camilo will understand their problem, not just deliver code.

Both groups convert to a conversation (email, LinkedIn, call), not a purchase. The portfolio's job is to make that first message feel natural rather than formal.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Eduard Camilo Sánchez García, a backend and full-stack developer based in Colombia working remotely. Built on Laravel + Tailwind CSS.

Success is a recruiter forwarding the link internally or a founder writing a first message within 48 hours of visiting. Failure is a clean, professional page that leaves no lasting impression.

## Brand Personality

Warm, direct, approachable.

References: leerob.io (real person, terse copy, no decoration, readable at a scan), cassie.codes (human warmth, personality in the details).

The portfolio should feel like it was written by the developer who built it: someone who has opinions, takes pride in their work, and is easy to talk to. Not a SaaS marketing page. Not a corporate resume.

## Anti-references

- **Generic dark SaaS landing page:** purple/blue gradient heroes, glowing card grids, feature-section layouts, "100% Commitment" stat cards, gradient text on headlines. This is the dominant aesthetic for AI tool landing pages in 2024-2025 and reads as template, not person.
- **GitHub-profile aesthetic:** contribution graphs, language badge grids, star counters, README-style layout. Looks like a config file, not a portfolio.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the person, not a product.** Camilo's voice, judgment, and context should come through. Every section should feel authored, not generated. A visitor should finish reading with a mental image of a specific person, not a job title.
2. **Warm before impressive.** Approachability opens conversations; technical credibility closes them. The hierarchy should lead with personality and context before listing stack and projects.
3. **Earned trust, no inflation.** No empty metrics, no inflated confidence claims, no decorative proof. Only state what can be felt or verified. One real project described honestly outweighs five vague ones.
4. **One clear next step.** Every section ends on a path toward contact. Recruiters and founders should never wonder what to do next.
5. **Readable at speed.** The page must communicate value in 20 seconds of scanning before inviting deeper reading. Hierarchy, measure, and spacing do the work; decoration does not.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the target. `prefers-reduced-motion` respected. Keyboard navigable with visible focus indicators. Form labels programmatically associated. Skip link in place. Semantic HTML throughout (`<nav>`, `<main>`, `<dl>/<dt>/<dd>` for key/value data, `<article>` for project entries).
